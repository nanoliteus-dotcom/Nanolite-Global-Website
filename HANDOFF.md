# Nanolite homepage — complete scene and camera handoff

This describes the 32 generated pictures and the intended continuous homepage journey. Use the pictures as visual references for a single animated world. They are key scenes, not consecutive animation frames. The mobile app is a separate project; this document covers the website visuals only.

## Overall direction

The visitor scrolls through a building being constructed, examines Nanolite materials in use, follows rainwater beneath a porous road, enters a manufacturing facility, inspects materials and machinery, then follows finished blocks back to the building. The journey ends with the completed building.

Keep the lake, mountains, vegetation, sunset direction, building footprint and factory identity consistent. Camera moves change what is visible; they do not replace the world with unrelated scenery. Materials should feel tactile: fine-pored concrete, matte white packaging, painted industrial machinery, soil and believable water.

Use a minimal interface: persistent Nanolite logo and navigation, concise section titles, short product descriptions, and one primary action where relevant. Text and buttons are separate interface elements, never baked into the pictures. Desktop product inspection generally places the subject on the left and information on the right; mobile places the information below the subject.

### Facts and reference corrections

- Brand spelling: **Nanolite**.
- Latest owner brief specifies **40 kg ReadyMix bags**, priced at **PKR 15/kg = PKR 600/bag** for Pakistan. Existing generated website scenes depict earlier 50 kg artwork; the supplied original artwork shows 20 kg. Keep the approved rectangular bag shape and graphic design, but reconcile the weight marking to the current 40 kg specification before publishing commerce content.
- Foaming-agent packaging in this visual sequence: **200-litre drums only**. Latest Pakistan price supplied: **PKR 1,000/litre**. Confirm whether sales must be in whole drums or may be dispensed in litres before implementing quantity selection.
- Other construction products use quote requests; plants use inquiries. Regional prices must be set explicitly rather than inferred from Pakistan prices.
- Plant names: **T-500, T-1000, T-5000, T-10000, T-15000**. Do not infer production capacities from their names.
- The owner explicitly requested white tanks for the white/blue mobile machines, including T-5000. Keep T-500 black/yellow.
- Generated tower pictures vary in floor count. Choose one approved high-rise model and preserve its floor count, footprint, stair position and structural grid across the animation. Scene 1–5 show the early stages of that same model.
- The factory arrangement is a concept, not a surveyed facility layout. Reference machinery pictures establish appearance, not engineering dimensions.
- Structural savings such as “20% less rebar” were suggested in the original brief. Publish exact savings only with approved supporting content; do not turn illustrative highlighting into an unsupported engineering claim.

## Scroll, interaction and layout behavior

1. Scroll drives a continuous sequence of distinct scene milestones. Blend positions, camera targets, lighting emphasis and interface visibility rather than snapping between pictures.
2. Give major camera changes enough travel to read, but do not make the visitor endure excessively long empty scrolling. Construction time-lapses can move faster than product-inspection holds.
3. Do not pull the visitor back upward or force them to wait for a title. Fast scrolling should move through the story smoothly. Text visibility should follow the relevant scene rather than an unrelated timer.
4. During an inspection, keep the product stable and readable. Product actions, navigation and an accessible route to forms remain usable. A selected rooftop product has a clear return control.
5. Separate continuous scroll from interactive actions: dragging a product preview must not accidentally change the scene. Mobile taps replace hover. Pointer focus, touch and keyboard navigation need equivalent routes.
6. Rain, splashes and underground water should have believable scale, reflections and direction. Avoid a flat bright line or mirror-like pavement across every scene. Treat the road drainage picture as a conceptual explanation.
7. Keep object ownership consistent: crane/panel remain connected, roof tile returns to its original position, block returns to the wall, parked machines remain in their bays, and the truck carries the same load across dispatch scenes.
8. Provide a reduced-motion experience with static scene images and ordinary scrolling. The user must still reach every product and form without animated travel.
9. These raster pictures are storyboard assets. Exact 360-degree orbits, passage through walls/roads, and consistent changing viewpoints require a coherent 3D scene or an approved rendered sequence. Moving and zooming flat pictures alone cannot reproduce those views faithfully.

## Commerce destinations attached to the story

**Order materials:** Image-led choice of ReadyMix and Foaming Agent → quantity → pickup/delivery → buyer/contact details → review → submit order request → reference/status screen. Use current pack size and regional prices. Show freight/tax as pending when unknown rather than silently zero.

**Construction quote:** Selected product → application/project location → quantity and relevant dimensions → plans/photos if available → contact → review/submit. Include an option for customers who do not know technical values.

**Plant/franchise inquiry:** Selected T model → business and contact → location/site readiness → desired output and available utilities if known → start period/franchise interest → attachments → review/submit. No invented checkout price for machinery.

**More information:** Product details and approved PDFs/catalogs are reachable through normal navigation as well as scene actions. The homepage is an introduction, not a requirement to watch the whole sequence before ordering.
