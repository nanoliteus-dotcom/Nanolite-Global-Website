Planned sequence / Actual sequence

Where the programme
departed from the plan.

The original sequence depended on advance funding and payment security being available before delivery. Those conditions did not arrive together.

01 · Advance funding

Planned

PKR 60m available at the funding outset, assumed as 17 December 2024 for this comparison.

Actual

The first recorded payment was PKR 2m on 17 December 2024. Gross receipts first crossed PKR 60m on 26 February 2026.

Consequence

The advance accumulated across 14 months and 9 days from the first receipt, rather than arriving as one initial funding package.

02 · Five PDCs

Planned

Five post-dated cheques for the remaining payment arrangement, handed over with the advance under Nanolite’s stated plan.

Actual

No PDCs received as of 14 November 2026, according to Nanolite management.

Consequence

Advance coverage did not complete the agreed cheque-security arrangement. Future installment security remains open.

03 · Delivery window

Planned

Three-month delivery and installation scenario: 17 March 2025 if full advance and PDCs had arrived on 17 December 2024.

Actual

The advance reference was covered on the reviewed reconciliation basis in February 2026. The PDC condition still remained unfulfilled.

Consequence

The assumed three-month sequence could not be treated as though its original funding and security conditions had been met.

04 · Procurement & logistics

Planned

Procurement, fabrication and shipping coordinated around the original programme and commercial assumptions.

Actual

The extended programme continued through inflation, carrying costs and further Gulf shipping disruption in 2026.

Consequence

Receipt of funding in February did not restore earlier supplier availability, freight conditions or the original cost base.

05 · Material business

Intended model

Ongoing Nanolite material supply to the CLC factory supported the expected long-term commercial recovery.

Actual

The material-supply agreement and NDA remain unsigned. The booklet proposes a Material Commitment Letter and direct supply framework.

Consequence

Future material purchases and revenue cannot yet be treated as secured. Machinery funding alone does not establish that future business.

The receipt independently documents a 6–8 business week shipment term after advance, five PDCs and four monthly installments starting 1 May 2025. The three-month installation scenario and exact PDC-at-outset timing are Nanolite’s stated planning basis.

How the shipping environment changed

2024

Existing Red Sea disruption. Yemen-linked attacks and diversions already affected maritime routes when the project began.

2025

Further regional escalation. June Iran–Israel tensions raised Hormuz concerns; merchant-vessel attacks resumed in the Red Sea in July.

28 Feb 2026

The next Gulf conflict phase. IMO dates the current conflict to two days after the advance funding milestone. This changed subsequent execution conditions.

Mar–Apr 2026

New carrier terms. Maersk published UAE emergency freight and contingency storage arrangements. Hapag-Lloyd documented alternative delivery via Khor Fakkan, including Umm Al Quwain.

Nov 2026

Continuing maritime risk. IMO’s 11 November statement records ongoing attacks. Normal shipping conditions cannot simply be assumed for every booking.

Published carrier example — not a Nanolite invoice

Maersk’s 17 March 2026 notice lists USD 3,000 emergency freight per 40-foot dry container on specified Gulf trades, including UAE. It also provides contingency storage terms. Applicability depends on the actual carrier, booking, route and cargo.

Responsibility & outstanding requirements

The sequence matters
when assessing delay.

Nanolite’s position is that the execution period must be assessed alongside the earlier funding shortfall, outstanding PDCs and the changed operating environment.

Ecolite payment obligations

  • The reviewed record shows staggered receipts instead of the full advance at the assumed outset.
  • Five PDCs remain unprovided, on Nanolite’s current account.
  • The receipt assigns destination taxes and customs-clearance charges to the franchisee.

These are the principal outstanding Ecolite-side commercial items identified in this review. Their effect on individual activities still requires a dated programme.

Nanolite execution obligations

  • Nanolite continues to affirm delivery and completion of the machinery project.
  • 8 months and 19 days elapsed between the February funding milestone and this review.
  • Remaining shipment, integration and commissioning steps need a jointly confirmed completion schedule.

Earlier funding delays do not, by themselves, explain every later day of execution. Nanolite’s subsequent actions must also be recorded and assessed.

Site & joint commercial requirements

  • Confirm equipment received, foundations, power, water and curing readiness.
  • Formalize material quantities, pricing, supply duration and payment terms.
  • Complete confidentiality documentation and agree the production/trial acceptance sequence.

Historical discussions report visa delays and corrective foundation work. Current status and any allocation of those delays require supporting records.

External operating conditions

  • Further 2026 regional conflict affected Gulf shipping conditions.
  • Carrier rerouting, emergency freight and storage terms changed logistics options.
  • Inflation and longer carrying periods put pressure on the original feasibility assumptions.

External disruption is documented. Its precise impact on this project’s route, costs and programme must be tied to bookings and invoices.

Nanolite’s central position

The delay should not be assessed solely from the date the advance was covered. The earlier funding sequence, continuing absence of PDCs, site dependencies and subsequent war-related disruption all belong in the review.

This identifies the parties’ open items and Nanolite’s commercial argument. It does not establish sole responsibility or a final contractual finding.

War, inflation and logistics: source detail

Regional maritime chronology

IMO shipping updates document attacks predating the project and renewed Red Sea attacks in July 2025. UNCTAD 2025 overview describes June 2025 Iran–Israel tensions around Hormuz.

IMO, 28 August 2026 dates the current conflict to 28 February. IMO, 11 November 2026 documents continuing maritime threats. No claim is made that every port or route was continuously closed.

Carrier cost mechanisms

Maersk, 17 March 2026 lists emergency freight of USD 1,800/20-foot dry and USD 3,000/40-foot dry on specified Gulf trades, including UAE, and contingent storage terms. These are dated carrier tariffs, not Nanolite invoices.

Hapag-Lloyd, 16 April 2026, pp.1–2 documents alternative routing via Khor Fakkan and UAE inland destinations, including Umm Al Quwain, with transport, war-risk and storage charges. The presentation does not combine different carriers’ tariffs into a project bill.

Inflation benchmarks

Government of Pakistan, 18 December 2024: November 2024 national CPI 4.9% year on year. PBS October 2026 monthly review and SBP October 2026 monitor: CPI 9.6% versus 6.2% in October 2025; WPI 13.8% year on year.

Annual snapshots are not added or compounded to estimate project inflation. CPI measures consumer prices; aggregate WPI is not a steel or machinery quotation.

Project-specific limits

Inflation, war-risk and carrier evidence support changed conditions. Measuring the impact requires like-for-like steel and machinery quotations, actual bookings, port/yard/warehouse bills, currency settlements and a dated programme.

Nanolite reports prolonged fabrication, payroll and storage exposure in earlier discussions. Historical estimates and disputed costs have not been converted into a verified loss or a demand in this presentation. No legal allocation of delay follows from these public sources alone.
